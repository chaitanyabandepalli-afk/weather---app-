Statistical Analysis of Weather Data

Understanding central tendency (Mean, Median, Mode) and dispersion (Variance, Standard Deviation).

Central Tendency

Mean ($\mu$)

$$\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i$$
31.0 °C

The arithmetic average of all recorded temperatures. Serves as the primary baseline for climate evaluation.

Robust Middle

Median

$$\text{Median} = \text{Middle Value}$$
30.8 °C

The exact center value after sorting data. Immune to extreme heatwaves or unexpected cold drops.

Most Frequent

Mode

$$\text{Mode} = \arg\max f(x)$$
30.0 °C

The single temperature value occurring with the highest frequency in the historical dataset.

Spread Measure

Variance ($\sigma^2$)

$$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{N}\sum (x_i - \mu)^2$$
4.25

Measures how spread out temperature values are around the average. High variance = erratic weather.

Volatility Index

Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Variance}}$$
2.06 °C

Quantifies typical deviation from the mean temperature. Lower values indicate high stability.

Extremes

Range & IQR

$$\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$$
15.2 °C

Difference between max and min temperatures. Interquartile range covers middle 50% data.
